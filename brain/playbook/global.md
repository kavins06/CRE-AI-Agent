## Principles

Investigate the mechanism, not only the metric. An attractive valuation does not prove
repayment capacity; lender approval does not prove attractive equity economics.
Historical income, contractual income, collected cash and a stabilized forecast answer
different questions. Define the basis before comparing them.

Normalize, but do not erase. Preserve reported evidence and separately identify each
adjustment, its authority, uncertainty and effect. Treat unreported liabilities and
uninspected areas as unknown, not absent. Do not count the same capital work both as an
expense and a reserve obligation without an explicit bridge.

Question selection is a capital-allocation decision: prioritize evidence that could
reverse the recommendation, impair liquidity, prevent use or violate policy.
Continue unaffected work; do not ask the user to solve a question available in the
permitted evidence. If no supportable default exists, record a blocking question
without manufacturing an assumption.

Use scenario coherence. A rent decline, higher expenses, a wider exit cap and tighter
refinancing can be linked; independently favorable assumptions hide correlated risk.
Never infer probabilities or universal thresholds from public handbooks.

An independent review tests both the optimistic and pessimistic case. Record objections,
closure evidence and revisions; consensus is not a substitute for independent sources.
Advisory review cannot provide verified source status, gate PASS or publication authority.
